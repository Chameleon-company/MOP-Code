# AS/NZS 1158.3.1:2020 — Pedestrian Area (Category P) Lighting

**Source:** AS/NZS 1158.3.1:2020 (Third edition, supersedes AS/NZS 1158.3.1:2005),
*Lighting for roads and public spaces, Part 3.1: Pedestrian area (Category P) lighting — Performance and design requirements*.

This document quotes the maintained light-technical parameters verbatim from Tables 3.3 through 3.7 of the 2020 standard. Where this knowledge base differs from older summaries of AS/NZS 1158, the 2020 numbers below are authoritative.

## Overview

The 2020 edition replaces the legacy P1–P12 category labels with **five subcategory families**, each addressing a distinct type of public space:

| Family | Applies to | Table |
|---|---|---|
| **PP1–PP5** | Pedestrian / cycle pathways, including park paths, footpaths, walkways, lanes | 3.4 |
| **PR1–PR6** | Local roads and access streets in residential, commercial, industrial precincts | 3.3 |
| **PA1–PA3** | Public activity areas — civic squares, malls, transport interchanges | 3.5 |
| **PC1–PC3** | Outdoor off-road car parks (including roof-top) | 3.7 |
| **PE1–PE3** | Connecting elements — subways, stairways, ramps, footbridges | 3.6 |

Each subcategory specifies four maintained light-technical parameters (LTPs):

- $E_h$ — average horizontal illuminance (lux)
- $E_{Ph}$ — point horizontal illuminance (lux)
- $\text{UE2}$ — illuminance uniformity, expressed as **max / avg** (lower = more uniform). This is a change from the pre-2020 standard, which used $E_{min} / E_{avg}$ (higher = better).
- $E_{Pv}$ — point vertical illuminance (lux), for facial recognition / CCTV effectiveness

## General design rules

- **Spacing.** Pole spacing is typically 3 to 5 times the mounting height; brighter subcategories require closer spacing for tighter UE2. A 4 m pole on a PP3 path → ~16 m spacing; on a PP1 path → ~12 m spacing.
- **Light source.** Minimum CRI 60 for all categories; higher subcategories (PP1–PP2, PA1–PA2) may require CRI ≥ 70.
- **Colour temperature.** For Melbourne, City of Melbourne ecological guidelines recommend 3000 K (warm white) to minimise impact on wildlife and align with the Urban Forest Strategy.
- **Maintenance factor.** Modern LED luminaires: LDD × LLD ≈ 0.85 to 0.90; older HPS technology: ~0.70.
- **Vertical illuminance.** $E_{Pv}$ is specified for higher subcategories to ensure facial recognition for pedestrian safety and CCTV effectiveness.
- **Lumen derating.** For PR3–PR6 and PP4–PP5 with light sources where S/P ratio < 1 or CCT < 2500 K, apply the multipliers in Clause 3.2.2.

## Subcategory selection for Melbourne park paths

For a moderate-use park path (e.g. Fitzroy Gardens main diagonal, Royal Botanic Gardens main loop):

- Use **PP3** (3 lx average) as the default.
- Upgrade to **PP2** (7 lx) if fear-of-crime is elevated for the LGA, or if the path is a primary commuter route.
- Upgrade to **PP1** (10 lx) for major entries, gathering nodes, or paths near transit interchanges.
- Downgrade to **PP4** (1.5 lx) only for quiet, low-traffic paths in low-risk areas — though Melbourne City Council typically does not specify below PP3 for paths with any evening use.

## Adaptive dimming

The standard allows dimming during low-activity periods, provided the dimmed level still satisfies the next-lower subcategory's requirement.

Example dimming schedules:

- A path designed to PP2 (7 lx) may dim to PP3 (3 lx) after midnight.
- A path designed to PP3 (3 lx) may dim to PP4 (1.5 lx) overnight.

This supports the energy performance methods in Appendix F (Power Density Indicator and Annual Energy Consumption Indicator) which assume variable dim states during the night.

## Note on the 2005 → 2020 transition

The 2020 edition's significant technical changes (per its Preface) include:

1. Separation of the previous Table 2.6 into the differing requirements for "local roads" and "cyclist paths".
2. Clarified glare requirements for HID luminaires and new requirements for SSL light sources.
3. Additional and revised layout rules for typical road layouts.
4. New requirements for surround illuminance ($E_{PhS}$) on pathways.
5. Additional lighting level for off-road car park areas.
6. New requirements for energy performance measurement and reporting (Appendix F).
7. Updated ingress-protection and maintenance regime requirements.

Earlier project iterations of this knowledge base used the 2005-edition P1–P12 labelling, which has been superseded.
